It hit me in the middle of the work. The meetings are the prompts.

I run a lot of them. Hour-long calls with teams, and they are dense. There is what a person says, and under it there is what they meant, and around it there is the thing nobody quite said but everyone heard. A tangent that turns out to be the real problem. A name dropped once. A question left hanging because we ran out of time. An hour of that is not an hour of talk. It is an hour of signal, layered, foreground and middle and background, and almost all of it is worth acting on.

And here is what we do with it. We take notes. Somebody writes a summary. The summary gets turned into a handful of action items, and the action items get handed out, and everyone goes off to work. That is the shape I have used my whole career, and it is the shape almost everyone uses.

## The compression was the point, and the compression was the loss

Think about what the notes step was actually for. A person cannot hold an hour of layered conversation in their head and act on all of it. So we compress. We throw away ninety percent and keep the five bullets a human can carry into next week. The summary is not a record of the meeting. It is a survival mechanism for human memory.

The problem is that the ninety percent we throw away is the richest part. The undertone, the tangent, the half-question, the thing someone almost said. That is exactly the material that turns into good research, good product, the second idea that was better than the first. We have spent decades feeding the most valuable output of a meeting into the trash, because we had no one who could carry it.

> The summary was never a record of the meeting. It was a workaround for the fact that a person cannot hold an hour in their head.

Now we have something that can hold the whole hour. So the workaround is not just unnecessary. It is the wrong move.

## The new shape

Stop compressing. The raw meeting, the whole recording, the whole transcript, undertones and tangents and all, becomes the prompt. Every person in the room takes it and loads it, whole, into their own setup. The way I load things into mine. No summary in between. No one deciding in advance which five things mattered.

The meeting is a single high-bandwidth signal that everyone was in the room for. What each person does with it is theirs. My infrastructure already knows my context, my protocols, my deliverables, the shape of my work. So when I drop the whole hour into it, it does not need me to have pre-chewed the hour into tasks. It reads the hour against everything it already holds about me, and the work just starts.

## The AI is one cog

This only works because of a thing I keep coming back to. The AI is not the machine. The AI is one cog in a machine that each of us has to build. I have a wheel. Folders, protocols, context, memory, the whole accumulated setup of how I work. The AI serves one function inside that wheel. It is powerful, but it is a part, not the whole.

Which means everyone needs their own wheel. Their own infrastructure, tuned to them. This is the part people miss when they think adopting AI is a matter of buying a seat. The seat is a cog. The wheel is the work. A meeting-as-prompt is useless dropped into nothing. It comes alive dropped into a person's own built-up infrastructure, because that infrastructure is what turns a shared hour into that person's specific next move.

## What actually changes

What changes is who decides what mattered. In the old shape, one person, the notetaker, the manager, decided which five things were worth keeping and handed the same five to everyone. Everyone got one person's read of the hour.

In the new shape nobody decides for everyone. The same full hour goes to five people, and each person's own infrastructure surfaces what matters to them, because it knows what they are carrying. The designer's setup pulls the design thread. Mine pulls mine. The salience is personal, and it is decided at the edge, by each person's own wheel, not at the center by one tired notetaker. Nothing gets thrown away, because no one had to choose what to throw away.

The meeting stops being a thing you recover from and starts being a thing you run on. It is not an interruption to the work that then has to be transcribed back into the work. It is the fuel, handed to everyone whole, at full richness, at once.

## The prerequisite

There is a cost, and it is the honest part. This only works if everyone has actually built their own infrastructure, and almost no one has yet. If your setup is empty, the whole meeting drops into nothing and nothing comes back. The work is not learning to summarize better. The work is that every person builds their own wheel, so that when the hour arrives it lands on something that knows what to do with it.

That is the shift. Not better notes. No notes. The meeting is the prompt, and the wheel each of us built is what reads it. It is a different shape, and once you see it you cannot go back to the old one.
