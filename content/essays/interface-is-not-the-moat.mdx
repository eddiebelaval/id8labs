## The Signal

In early 2026, Salesforce — a company that built a trillion-dollar empire on owning the screen — went headless.

They stripped their UI from their logic layer and exposed their CRM as callable infrastructure. After decades of betting on the interface as the product, they quietly admitted: **the interface is a commodity. The engine underneath is the moat.**

This is not a small move. This is Salesforce reading the same water every AI-native builder has been reading for two years.

And it has direct implications for Homer.

---

## What "Headless" Actually Means

Headless doesn't mean invisible. It means **decoupled**.

The intelligence layer — the logic, the data models, the reasoning — exists independently of any single surface. Other things call it. Browsers call it. Agents call it. CLIs call it. Partner platforms call it.

The product stops being "the app you open" and becomes "the layer everything plugs into."

Think of it like municipal water infrastructure. You don't interact with the pipes. You turn on a tap — whatever tap you're standing near — and the water arrives. The pipes don't care about the tap.

Homer's Deal State Machine, SLA engine, comp analysis, and translation layer are the pipes. The consumer UI is one tap. An agent call is another. A partner API integration is another.

---

## Homer's Natural Architecture

Homer was designed — intentionally or by instinct — to be headless-ready.

The core intelligence doesn't live in the UI. It lives in:

- **The Deal State Machine** — tracks where every deal is, what's outstanding, what's at risk
- **The SLA Engine** — enforces timing, triggers, and accountability across both sides
- **Comp Analysis** — real-time market intelligence against live inventory
- **The Translation Layer** — converts complex real estate process into plain language any caller can use

None of these require a browser. They require a caller.

The consumer UI we've built is the first caller. It won't be the last.

---

## Three Caller Scenarios

**1. Partner Integrations**

Brokerages, portals, and media platforms (think: Miami Deal Sheet, local MLS adjacents) want Homer's intelligence without building their own. They embed Homer as a capability. They render however they want. Homer runs underneath.

**2. Agent-to-Agent**

As AI agents proliferate, they need tools. An AI concierge helping someone find a home shouldn't have to rebuild deal intelligence from scratch — it should call Homer. Homer becomes an MCP tool, a function call, a capability in someone else's agent stack.

**3. Internal CLI / Dev Tooling**

Pod members, developers, and operators run Homer queries from the terminal. Batch operations. Testing. Pipeline inspection. No UI needed.

---

## The Sequencing Question

Going headless too early is a trap.

The consumer surface needs to be proven first — not because the UI is the product, but because **the UI is how you validate the intelligence layer**. Real users stress-testing real deals tells you whether the engine is sound.

The right trigger for headless: **a second caller that isn't the Homer UI.**

When someone outside the product wants to call Homer's logic, that's the market telling you the pipes are worth plumbing.

---

## Defensive vs. Offensive Headless

Salesforce's move is defensive. They spent decades building walls around their UI. Now the walls don't protect anything. Going headless is them adapting — repositioning to survive a distribution shift they didn't author.

Homer can do something different: **be born headless-ready.**

Not retrofitted. Designed with the assumption that the UI is one expression of a deeper layer, not the layer itself.

That's a fundamentally different energy. One company is retreating to infrastructure. The other is building infrastructure as the founding thesis.

---

## The Broader Pattern

What Salesforce is signaling — and what the AI-native moment is accelerating — is a full inversion of where value lives in software.

For thirty years: **UI = product = moat.**

Now: **Intelligence layer = product. UI = distribution channel.**

The companies that internalize this early don't just survive the shift. They become the pipes that the next generation of surfaces is built on top of.

Homer's opportunity isn't just to be a good real estate app. It's to be **the real estate intelligence layer that AI agents call when they need to understand a deal.**

That's a different category entirely.
