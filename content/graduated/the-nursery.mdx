A studio that builds for one client can hold the whole thing in its head. A studio that builds for many cannot. Every engagement leaves a growing pile of artifacts. Every brand is identity, and identity drifts. A studio that keeps taking clients drops them one at a time, quietly, not from malice but from gravity. The Nursery is the answer to that gravity.

The question that named it came from a client. After a few weeks of receiving documents, hubs, runbooks, and a dashboard, Rose at Donato & Brill asked the plain version of it: how do I handle all these files you make me. The honest answer is not a better folder. It is a system that holds the work, watches it, and surfaces what has drifted, so the operator does not have to track it by hand.

## The stack underneath

The Nursery sits on top of four things built in the same session, each a layer of the same idea.

**The HALO** is the per-client envelope: the brand's Genome (its DESIGN, VOICE, and ETHOS), its engagements, and the surfaces built for it. A HALO is internal. The client receives the product; the studio keeps the source DNA. The HALO is the layer we build from.

**The Hub** is the HALO's front door: one gated surface that organizes everything we make for a client. It has a fixed six-section information architecture, so a client learns it once and knows every future one, and two faces off one skeleton: an internal command view and a clean client view.

**The vault** is where the fleet of HALOs lives. A separate, private repository, on purpose. A HALO carries the studio's operating doctrine, including its pricing posture and its ethos, so it must never sit in a repository that ships to a client. The vault is the safe home, and keeping HALOs out of shipping repositories is a rule the system enforces, not a habit it hopes for.

**The intake pattern** is how a brand becomes a resident. Two paths, one contract. A new brand is installed and its Genome filled. An existing brand, often found living inside a product or app repository where it could leak, is re-homed and reconciled. Both converge on the same bar: the HALO verifies complete, and only then is it registered.

## The model

The Nursery is a chain of primitives that tends the fleet. The model is a nursery in the literal sense.

The babies are the HALOs. Each is a living brand envelope.

The nurses are the primitives. Each does one thing well. They make rounds over the babies, checking health, watching for drift, bringing state current, raising quality, keeping hygiene. Most already existed as skills. The Nursery composes them into one scheduled pass over the whole fleet.

The rounds are the schedule. They run on their own, daily, read-only, and surface anything that needs a person.

The whole is the mother hen. Continuous custodianship. This is the studio's promise past the build, the retainer made concrete, and it is what lets one studio tend many brands without dropping any.

A HALO is alive because the Nursery tends it. Without the Nursery a HALO is just files.

## The nurses

The chain runs in order over each HALO. Two of the nurses gate the rounds. The rest surface work without failing them, because a brand that needs attention is not a brand that is broken.

| Nurse | Asks | Backed by |
|---|---|---|
| VERIFY | Is this HALO correct and complete? | a deterministic contract check |
| AUDIT | Has the brand moved? Is the drift audit current? | freshness plus the deep audit skill |
| EVAL | Can this HALO be graded? Does its output meet the bar? | a held-out golden set plus the grade-and-tune loop |
| RECONCILE | Does the engagement triad match reality? | triad presence plus the reconcile skill |
| MARKET ALIGNMENT | Is the brand still where the market is? | a quarterly research pass |
| CLEANUP | Any secrets, stray copies, or a HALO sitting in a repo that ships? | a leak check plus a secret scan |

The deep work in AUDIT, EVAL, and MARKET ALIGNMENT is judgment, not arithmetic: read the genes, sample the live work, research the category, grade against a held-out set. So the nurse does the deterministic half, whether the pass is current and whether the inputs exist, and routes the deep pass to a skill or a research agent. Nothing in the chain rewrites a brand on its own. Anything that would change identity is surfaced to the human gate.

## Two loops inside the chain

Two of the nurses are loops in their own right, and they are where the system earns its keep.

The **eval loop** is grade and tune. An eval is a held-out set of known-good examples plus a bar. Without one, a HALO can be asserted but not proven. The loop grades produced output on two axes, form parity against the Genome and accuracy against canonical truth. It compares the score to the bar and to the last run, and feeds the misses back. This is the CONFIRM loop, the return path first built inside a single client chain, lifted to the whole fleet. The EVAL nurse exists to surface the most common gap of all: a brand with no way to measure itself. On its first run it did exactly that, flagging both resident HALOs as not yet gradable.

The **market-alignment loop** is the outward eye. The audit asks an inward question: are we still ourselves. Market alignment asks the outward one: are we still where the market is. A brand can be perfectly self-consistent and quietly falling behind its category. The loop samples the market, diffs the brand against the frontier, classifies each axis as aligned, drifting behind, or ahead on purpose, and surfaces proposed moves. Run for real on the two residents, it found both aligned. Donato & Brill sits ahead on purpose on its design and voice, a deliberate bet held against the market. id8Labs is aligned and slightly ahead. Neither edge was a gap to close.

## Why it matters

The Nursery is the operational form of a claim the studio makes about itself: custodians, not vendors. A vendor hands off. A custodian stays. Staying is easy to promise and hard to do at scale. Tending many living things by hand does not scale, and a person forgets. The Nursery is what makes the promise keepable. It is the difference between a HALO that is a snapshot and a HALO that is alive, and the difference between a studio that can hold one client well and a studio that can hold a fleet.
